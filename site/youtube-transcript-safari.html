Ask a YouTube video what was said, in Safari

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

You don't want to watch 40 minutes to find the two that matter. In Safari on iPhone, iPad or Mac, ContextFlow reads the video's transcript and answers from it, pointing to the moment, like [4:15]. I make ContextFlow, so I'm not neutral. Two things up front: it needs Pro, and it needs the video to have captions.

How it works

  • Open the video on the YouTube website in Safari. On iPhone, YouTube's mobile site works too.
  • Tap the ContextFlow button, then Summarize, or ask your own question: “what did she say about pricing?”, “list the tools he mentions”.
  • The answer comes from the transcript and points to the moments it's from, like [4:15].
  • Videos on other sites work too when they use the standard web video player with a captions file.

What it needs

  • Captions. ContextFlow reads the captions the video's player loads, the same ones you'd turn on with CC. If a video has none, the answer uses its title and description, and says so.
  • Pro. On Free, answers use what the page shows: the title and the description, not the transcript.
  • The YouTube website, not the YouTube app: ContextFlow lives in Safari.

Free ways first

  • YouTube's own transcript: on youtube.com, open the description and choose Show transcript. It's free and gives you the full text with timestamps, but you read it yourself.
  • Transcript websites: paste a link, get the text. If you want the words themselves, to copy or to save, they're the right tool.
  • ContextFlow is for when you want an answer, not a wall of text: the gist, or one part of the video, with the moment it's from.

Where it falls short

  • It doesn't hand you the full transcript as a file: it answers from it.
  • A very long video, like a three-hour podcast, is read only in part.
  • YouTube videos embedded in other sites and players that draw their own captions, like Vimeo's, aren't covered.
  • When YouTube changes its pages, reading the transcript can fail for a while. Then the answer says so and uses the title and description.

Questions

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes, in Safari on the YouTube website, on iPhone, iPad and Mac. It doesn't work inside the YouTube app.

What if the video has no captions?

Then the answer uses the video's title and description, and ContextFlow tells you it had no transcript to read.

Can I get the whole transcript as text?

Not as a file: ContextFlow answers from the transcript. For the full text, open the description on youtube.com and choose Show transcript.

Is it free?

Questions about the page are free to start, but reading the video's transcript is Pro. On Free, answers use the video's title and description.
