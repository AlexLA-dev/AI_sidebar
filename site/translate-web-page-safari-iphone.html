Translate a web page in Safari on iPhone, links and all

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

You land on a page in German, Japanese or Italian and want to read it in your language without losing your place. Safari can often translate it for free from the page menu, so I'll start there. When Safari doesn't offer to, for that language or where you live, or when you want to ask about the page next, ContextFlow translates in place too. I make ContextFlow, so I'm not neutral.

Safari's own translation

  • Open the page menu in the address bar and choose Translate to your language. The page is translated where it is, and its links keep working.
  • Good: free, built in, one tap.
  • Limits: it shows only when Safari can translate the page's language, and it isn't offered in every country or region. If it's not in the menu, Safari can't translate that page for you.

Translating in place with ContextFlow

  • On a page in another language, a button shows by ✦ and offers to translate it into your iPhone's language. Tap Translate.
  • The page reads in your language in its own layout. What's on screen comes first, the rest as you scroll, and text that loads later is translated as it comes.
  • Links stay links. They open the same pages they did, so you can keep reading the site. Bold and italics stay too.
  • Original brings back every word of the page.
  • Menus, buttons and tabs stay as they are, so the site still works the way it was built.
  • Then ask about the page in your own language: Summarize, or a question of your own.
  • Don't need it on a site? The × hides it there.

Which languages

  • From: whatever language the page is in. ContextFlow tells it by the page's own text, so a Persian blog or a Polish forum gets the button as well as a German news site.
  • Into: your iPhone's language, the one Safari uses.

What it costs

  • Each page you translate counts as one answer. Free has 5 answers a day; Pro has up to 375 a week, for $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year.
  • Coming back to a page you translated that week doesn't count again.

Which to pick

  • Safari offers to translate the page: take it. It's free and it's right there.
  • Safari doesn't offer it, for that page, that language or where you live: ContextFlow's button.
  • You want to ask about the page next, in your own language: ContextFlow, since its panel opens right on that page.

Where it falls short

  • It's an AI model. Now and then a word stays untranslated or comes out odd; Original is one tap away.
  • The first lines take a second or two, and a long page fills in as you scroll.
  • Text inside pictures stays as it is.
  • The page's text goes to the AI to be translated, as it does for any question. It isn't kept on my server.

Questions

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes, in Safari on iPhone, iPad and Mac. The button shows by ✦ on pages in a language other than yours.

Do links still work after the page is translated?

Yes. Every link keeps its address and opens the same page as before. Original puts the page back exactly as it was.

Is it free?

Each page you translate counts as one answer, and Free has 5 a day. Pro has up to 375 answers a week. Safari's own translation is free when it offers it.

Which languages can it translate?

From whatever language the page is in, into your iPhone's language, the one Safari uses.
