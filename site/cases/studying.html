Cases › Studying a topic

Learning something from too many pages?

A course, a thesis, a new job, or a topic you just want to understand. The reading piles up: long articles, lecture videos, PDFs, and tabs you meant to come back to.

Here's how ContextFlow helps with each of them, right on the page in Safari.

Is this article worth an hour?

Open ContextFlow on it and tap Summarize or Key takeaways. You get the gist and the numbers that matter, and then decide whether to read the whole thing. Explain simply helps when it's written for experts.

Summarize, Key takeaways, Explain simply, free.

One paragraph makes no sense

Select it and choose Ask AI. The answer is about that part only, with the rest of the page in mind. It can explain it, translate it or say it more simply. A source in another language? Select a paragraph and choose Translate, or ask about it in your own language.

Questions about a selection, free.

A lecture video, and you need one part of it

ContextFlow reads the video's transcript. Ask for the gist, or what was said about one thing, and the answer points to the moment, like [4:15], so you jump there instead of scrubbing.

Questions about a video Pro. On Free, answers use the page's text: the title and the description.

The paper is a PDF

Papers and reports are often PDFs, shown on a page or linked from it. Those go with your question, and the answer says which page of the PDF it's from, so checking is quick. On iPad and Mac it works on a PDF tab too.

Questions about a PDF Pro. On Free, answers use the page's text.

Pulling it together before the exam

Highlight the line that matters in an answer, and write your own notes as you read. Then turn on Whole chat and ask “What are the main arguments so far?”: the answer uses every page, answer and note in this chat. With Pro, the first row of the chat list answers from all your chats at once.

Highlights, notes, Whole chat, free. Ask your chats Pro

No time to sit and read

Choose Listen to this page in the … menu and hear the article on the way, or tap Listen under a summary. A natural voice reads it, in the page's language, at the speed you pick. On iPhone it keeps playing with the screen locked.

Listen Ultra

Before you rely on it

  • AI can be wrong. Check anything you'll quote or hand in. Answers about a PDF say which page they're from, so you can look.
  • Video questions work on YouTube and on pages whose videos come with standard captions. Players that draw their own captions aren't covered.
  • Safari doesn't let extensions read some pages, like the start page. You can still ask general questions there.
  • If you only need the gist now and then, Safari's own summary may be enough. I compared them in Safari's summary vs ChatGPT vs an extension.

Questions

Does it work on pages I'm signed in to, like my university's?

Yes. It reads the page as it is in your Safari, so a course page you're signed in to works too.

Can I keep notes for a whole course?

Yes. Keep one chat per course or topic: notes stay next to the pages they came from. Tag the chats to group them, or turn on Folders. More in keeping notes while you read.

Which videos can it read?

Videos on YouTube, and videos on other pages that come with standard captions. It reads the transcript, so a video without captions has nothing to read.

What's free and what's Pro?

Summaries, questions, highlights, notes and Whole chat work on Free, with a daily allowance of answers. Videos, PDFs, AI search and questions over all your chats need Pro. Listening is part of Ultra.
