Price and stock alerts on iPhone: Visualping, Distill or Safari

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

You're on a product page on your iPhone and you want to hear when the price drops or it's back in stock. Visualping and Distill are the best-known page watchers, but they're built around their own servers or a computer. Safari now has a Notify Me of its own. I make ContextFlow, which watches from inside Safari, so I'm not neutral. Here's how the four compare.

Visualping

  • Watches a whole page or one part of it, on Visualping's servers, so the checks run with your phone off.
  • Free: 5 pages and 150 checks a month, at most once an hour. Paid plans start at $14 a month ($10 billed yearly) and check as often as every 2 minutes on the bigger ones (Visualping's pricing).
  • Alerts by email and webhook; SMS from the Personal 5K plan, US and Canadian numbers only.
  • You set watches up in its web app or Chrome extension. I couldn't find an iPhone app or a Safari extension.

Distill

  • You select the part of the page to watch. Checks run in its extension or desktop app on your computer, as often as every 5 seconds, or on Distill's servers.
  • Free: 25 monitors, 5 of them in the cloud, with cloud checks at most every 6 hours and 1,000 a month. Paid plans start at $15 a month, with cloud checks every 10 minutes (Distill's pricing).
  • Its iPhone app shows your watchlist and sends push alerts. The checks themselves run on Distill's servers or on your computer, not on the phone.
  • Extensions for Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Opera; none for Safari. Alerts by push and email, SMS on paid plans.

Safari's own Notify Me

  • Safari in iOS 27 and macOS 27 has Notify Me: choose it from the page menu, describe what to look for, like a price change, and Safari tells you when the page updates (Apple's guide).
  • It needs Apple Intelligence: on iPhone, a 15 Pro or later, set to a supported language. Apple says usage limits may apply (About iOS 27).
  • If your iPhone has it, try it first: it's built in and free.

ContextFlow

  • On the product page in Safari, select the price, the stock status or a date and tap the eye. Or choose Watch this page… in the … menu and say what you're waiting for, like “below $150” or “back in stock in size M”.
  • It checks each page at most every 15 minutes, from Safari on your device, and tells you with a notification. It works on any store's site you open in Safari, small shops included, with no store account.
  • Free watches 5 pages at once, Pro 20 and Ultra 50. It works without Apple Intelligence.
  • The catch: it runs while Safari runs. On iPhone you may hear about a change once you're back in Safari, not the minute it happens. A few sites don't let it check in the background; those it checks when you open the page.

The same button answers questions about the product page too, like what the reviews keep saying. More in shopping in Safari.

Which to pick

  • You need to know within minutes, with your phone in your pocket, or by email or SMS: Visualping, or Distill's cloud checks. They run on servers.
  • You have a computer that's always on: Distill's extension or desktop app checks as often as you like, free.
  • A few prices, set up in two taps on your iPhone: Safari's Notify Me if your iPhone has Apple Intelligence, or ContextFlow if it doesn't, or if you also want to ask the page what the catch is.

Questions

Is there a Visualping app for iPhone?

I couldn't find one, or a Safari extension. Visualping's checks run on its servers, so its emails reach you even with your phone off; you set watches up in its web app or Chrome extension.

Why does ContextFlow sometimes tell me late on iPhone?

It checks from Safari, and watching runs while Safari runs. On iPhone you may hear about a change once you're back in Safari, not the minute it happens.

Does it work on any store?

On store sites you open in Safari, yes, small shops included, with no store account. A few sites don't let it check in the background; those it checks when you open the page.
