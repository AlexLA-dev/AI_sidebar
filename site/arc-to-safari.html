Moving from Arc to Safari

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

Arc's own words: “Arc is sunsetting on December 22, 2026” (arc.net). After that date it won't open on Mac or Windows, and accounts are deleted within 30 days. The team's answer is Dia. If you'd rather move to Safari, which is already on your Mac and iPhone, here's how to bring your setup over and what you'll have to leave behind. Almost all of it is Safari on its own; one step mentions ContextFlow, which I make.

1. Before December 22: save what Arc can export

  • Spaces and tabs: in Arc, choose Arc › Settings, then Advanced, then Export Data. Arc saves a file named Arc Data Export.md, with a section for each Space listing its Favorites, pinned tabs, folders and other tabs as links (Arc's help article).
  • It's a list of links, not a bookmarks file, so Safari can't import it. Open it in any text editor and open the links you want to keep in Safari (step 2 shows where to put them). For one folder at a time, right-click it in Arc's sidebar and choose Copy All Links.
  • Easels: open your Library, select Easels, then Export All Easels. You get a ZIP with every Easel you made as a web page that opens in any browser. Easels others shared with you aren't in it.
  • Boosts: Arc says they can't be exported and don't come over to Dia either. If you wrote code in a Boost, copy it into a note before December 22.
  • Passwords: Arc's export doesn't include them. If you keep them in iCloud Keychain or a password manager, they're already outside Arc. If they're only in Arc, look for an export option in its password manager (arc://password-manager), then import the file into Safari as in step 4.

2. Spaces → profiles or Tab Groups

  • A Space for work and one for home map best to Safari profiles: Safari › Settings › Profiles. Each profile keeps its own history, cookies, website data, extensions, Tab Groups and favorites. Passwords in iCloud Keychain work in every profile (Apple's guide).
  • A Space per project maps to a Tab Group, which is lighter and syncs to your iPhone and iPad through iCloud. Open the Space's links in a window, then click the arrow next to the sidebar button and choose New Tab Group with N Tabs (N is how many are open).
  • Arc's folders can become bookmark folders: with the tabs open, choose Bookmarks › Add Bookmarks for These N Tabs.

3. Pinned tabs and Favorites

  • Pinned tabs: drag a tab to the left end of the tab bar, or choose Window › Pin Tab. Pinned tabs stay put when you open a new window or quit and reopen Safari. On iPhone and iPad, touch and hold a tab in the tab overview and choose Pin Tab.
  • Favorites: the icons at the top of Arc's sidebar become Safari's Favorites. Each profile picks its own bookmarks folder for them.

4. Import files into Safari

  • On a Mac, choose File › Import Browsing Data from File or Folder, and pick the file: bookmarks, passwords or history exported from another browser (Apple's guide).
  • Safari imports straight from Chrome and Firefox, but not from Arc.
  • Delete the exported file afterwards. Apple's warning: it may include unencrypted data.

5. Arc Max → asking the page in Safari

  • Arc had already dropped Ask on Page: its release notes for August 28, 2025 say “Ask On Page has been removed from Arc Max” (Arc's release notes). Tidy Tab Titles and Tidy Downloads stayed until the end.
  • Safari on its own: Summarize in Reader, on Macs and iPhones with Apple Intelligence. It gives you a summary, not answers to your questions.
  • To ask questions about the page you're reading: ContextFlow, the extension I make, answers about the page or a part you select, sums it up and translates it, on Mac, iPhone and iPad. It isn't Arc Max: it doesn't preview links before you open them, and it doesn't rename tabs or downloads.

What you won't get in Safari

  • Easels. Save them as in step 1; Safari has nothing like them.
  • Boosts. Nothing built in. The closest thing is a userscript extension such as Userscripts, free and open source, in the App Store for Mac, iPhone and iPad, which runs your own CSS or JavaScript on a site.
  • Split view inside one window. Safari doesn't have it. On a Mac, put two Safari windows side by side; on an iPad, open two Safari windows in Split View.

Or move to Dia

If you want Arc's way of working as it was, Dia is the closer pick. Arc's site says Dia brings over your passwords, history, tabs, Profiles, Folders, bookmarks and extensions, on Mac and Windows. It has no iPhone app on its site, and its AI features come with paid plans (Dia's pricing).

Questions

When does Arc stop working?

On December 22, 2026. Arc's site says you will no longer be able to access or use Arc on macOS or Windows from that day. Accounts are deactivated, and their personal data is deleted within 30 days.

Can Safari import straight from Arc?

No. Safari imports straight from Chrome and Firefox, and from exported files. Arc's export is a list of links, so you open or bookmark them in Safari yourself.

Do I need ContextFlow for any of this?

No. Everything here except asking questions about a page is Safari on its own.
