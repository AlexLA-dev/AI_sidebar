Listen to a web page in Safari on iPhone

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

You'd rather hear that long article on a walk than read it on a small screen. There are three ways to do that in Safari on iPhone, and two of them are free. I make the third, ContextFlow, so weigh that.

Safari's own Listen to Page

  • Since iOS 17, Safari can read an article aloud: open the page menu in the address bar and choose Listen to Page.
  • Good: free and built in, nothing to install.
  • Limits: it's greyed out on some pages, and it reads the page as it is, word for word.

Speak Screen

  • Turn it on in Settings › Accessibility › Spoken Content › Speak Screen. Then swipe down with two fingers from the top of the screen, and iPhone reads what's on it.
  • Good: free, and it works in any app, not only Safari.
  • Limits: it reads everything on the screen, menus and buttons included.

ContextFlow's Listen

  • In the ContextFlow panel, choose Listen to this page in the … menu, or tap Listen under an answer.
  • A natural voice in 100+ languages, at 1×, 1.2×, 1.5× or 2×. Tables, lists and code are rewritten for the ear first.
  • The screen can be locked. On iPhone and iPad, Listen plays in the ContextFlow app, so it keeps going with the screen locked and in your headphones. On a Mac, it plays right in the panel.
  • A summary instead of the whole page: tap Summarize first, then Listen under the answer.
  • Ultra: 30 minutes of listening a week. Hearing something again that's already been voiced is free. $12.99 a month or $119.99 a year.

Which to pick

  • An article read to you, free: Safari's Listen to Page.
  • Anything on the screen, in any app: Speak Screen.
  • A natural voice in your language, answers read aloud too, or a summary read to you instead of the whole thing: ContextFlow's Listen, with Ultra.

Questions

Does ContextFlow keep reading with the screen locked?

Yes. On iPhone and iPad, Listen plays in the ContextFlow app, so it keeps going with the screen locked and in your headphones. Allow notifications, and it carries on when you leave Safari; otherwise Listen opens the app.

How much listening does Ultra include?

30 minutes a week. Hearing something again that's already been voiced is free, and the panel shows how many minutes are left.

Does it need the internet?

Yes, the first time something is read. The voice is made online.
