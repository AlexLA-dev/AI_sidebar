Check what you write in Safari, with the page in mind

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

A typo in someone's name, a reply that misses what they asked. Apple's Writing Tools on newer devices and Grammarly both check what you write, so I'll start with those. What they don't see is the page you're answering: the email, the post, the form. ContextFlow's Check and Rewrite read it too. I make ContextFlow, so I'm not neutral.

Apple's Writing Tools

  • On iPhone 15 Pro and newer, and on iPad and Mac with M1 or later, with Apple Intelligence on: select your text and choose Writing Tools, then Proofread, or Rewrite it to sound friendly, professional or concise.
  • Good: free, and in every app, not only Safari. iOS 27 adds automatic proofreading.
  • Limits: it needs one of those devices and a language Apple Intelligence supports. Russian, Ukrainian and Arabic aren't among them yet.

Grammarly

  • Grammarly has a keyboard for iPhone and iPad, and a Safari extension.
  • Good: its keyboard works in every app, with suggestions on clarity and tone.
  • Limits: you sign in with a Grammarly account, and the fuller suggestions are in its paid plan.

ContextFlow's Check and Rewrite

  • In a text field on a website in Safari: a reply in Gmail, a comment on LinkedIn, a job application, a support ticket.
  • On Mac, a circle shows above the field once you've typed a couple of words; point at it for Check and Rewrite. On iPhone and iPad, tap the ContextFlow button by the field: they're at the top of the list.
  • It reads the page. The email you're answering, or the post under your comment. So the person's name comes out as they spell it, and a rewrite fits what you're replying to.
  • It sounds like you. It takes your About me, your templates and what you wrote on that site before. Those earlier texts stay on this device.
  • Check changes only the words that need it, so your signature, quotes, links and bold stay. Rewrite makes it shorter, warmer, more formal or more confident. Undo is one tap.
  • Underlines as you type Pro: pause, and the paragraph you just changed is checked with the page in mind. Point at an underline, or tap it on iPhone and iPad, then Fix or Ignore.
  • It never runs on password, card or payment fields, in code editors or in Google Docs.

What it costs

  • Each tap on Check or Rewrite counts as one answer. Free has 5 answers a day; Pro has up to 375 a week, for $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year.
  • Underlines as you type are in Pro and Ultra, and they don't count as answers.

Which to pick

  • Your device has Apple Intelligence, in a language it supports: try Writing Tools first. It's free and it's everywhere.
  • You rely on Grammarly in every app: keep it.
  • You answer people on websites, and the reply depends on what they wrote: ContextFlow reads that page. It also works on devices without Apple Intelligence, and in languages Apple Intelligence doesn't support yet.

Where it falls short

  • It lives in Safari. Not in Mail, Messages or Notes, and not in the ContextFlow app's own chat.
  • Not in Google Docs or code editors.
  • On Free, each tap uses one of 5 daily answers, so if you write a lot you'll want Pro.
  • It's an AI model. Read the result before you send it; Undo is right there.

Questions

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes, in Safari on iPhone, iPad and Mac, in text fields on websites. On iPhone and iPad, tap the ContextFlow button by the field; Check and Rewrite are at the top of the list.

Does it work in Gmail?

On the Gmail website in Safari, yes, and your signature stays as it was. Not in the Gmail app.

Is it free?

Each tap on Check or Rewrite counts as one answer, and Free has 5 a day. Underlines as you type are in Pro and Ultra.

Is what I write kept?

Your text and the page go to the AI to be checked, as with any question, and aren't kept on my server. Your earlier texts, used to match how you write, stay on this device.
