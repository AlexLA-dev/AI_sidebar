Summarize a PDF in Safari

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

The details are in a 40-page PDF and you need two of them. With Pro, ContextFlow reads PDFs in Safari and answers with the page each answer comes from. I make ContextFlow, so I'm not neutral, and I'll say where something else is the better pick.

How it works

  • A PDF on a web page, or linked from it: open the page, tap the ContextFlow button and ask. The PDFs shown or linked on the page go with your question.
  • A PDF open in its own tab: on iPad and Mac, ContextFlow works there too. On iPhone, ask from the page that links to it.
  • Answers say which page of the PDF they come from, so you can check.
  • Summarize, Key takeaways and Explain simply work on a PDF like on any page, and so do your own questions: “what's the cancellation fee?”, “what changed from last year?”.

What it can't read

  • Scanned PDFs. If the pages are pictures with no text, ContextFlow says so and answers without the PDF.
  • Very long PDFs are read only in part, so answers can miss what's near the end.
  • On Free, answers use the page's own text, not the PDFs on it.

When something else is better

  • A long session with one big PDF: uploading it to a chatbot app like ChatGPT or Claude gives you more room for back-and-forth. You download it and upload it first.
  • A PDF on your phone, not on a web page: ContextFlow works in Safari, not in the Files app.
  • A quick look in Safari: ContextFlow saves you the download and the upload, and keeps the answer in a chat with the page it came from.

Questions

Does it work on iPhone?

Yes, for PDFs shown on a web page or linked from it. A PDF open in its own tab works on iPad and Mac.

Can it read a scanned PDF?

No. If the pages are images with no text, ContextFlow says so and answers without the PDF.

Is it free?

Reading PDFs is Pro. On Free, answers use the page's own text, not the PDFs on it.
