Find a similar product cheaper while shopping in Safari on iPhone

Updated November 2026 · from the developer of ContextFlow

You're on a product page in Safari and wonder whether the same thing, or something close, costs less somewhere else, or is in stock. The common answer is Google Lens, so I'll start there. I make ContextFlow, so I'm not neutral: its Find similar does this from the product page, and below is where it differs.

Google Lens on a screenshot

  • Take a screenshot of the product, open it in the Google app or Google Lens, and look through what it finds.
  • Good: free, and good at things that look alike: clothes, furniture, a lamp you can't name.
  • Limits: you leave Safari and the page you were on. It matches the picture, not the model, the size or the specs. Prices and stock come from listings, so you open each shop yourself to see whether they're still right.

Find similar in ContextFlow

  • On the product page, tap ✦, then Find similar. Or select the product's name and tap Find similar under it.
  • Pick what you want: Cheaper, In stock or Newer. Or say it in your own words, like “same size, in black”.
  • It looks for alternatives in other shops, opens every page it finds and checks it before you see it, and shows the price and stock that page shows. A page it couldn't open is marked as such.
  • It reads the page you're on, so it goes by the model and the specs, not by how the picture looks.
  • Found one you like? Watch price follows it and tells you when the price changes.
  • It works on store sites you open in Safari, small shops included. It isn't tied to one marketplace.

Which plan has it

  • Ultra: the full search, 10 a week.
  • Pro and Own key: a quick search, 5 a week. On Pro each one counts as 10 answers.
  • Free: no Find similar, but you can still ask about the page, its reviews, or what to look out for.

Which to pick

  • You're after a look, a style or something you can't name: Google Lens.
  • You're after the same kind of thing, cheaper, in stock or newer, and want to know the shop's page really says so: Find similar.
  • You're going to wait for a better price: Watch price, on the product or on an alternative.

Where it falls short

  • It isn't a price comparison of every shop. It finds a handful of good alternatives and checks them.
  • Some shops don't let a page be opened from outside; those alternatives come without a check.
  • It's an AI model. Read the shop's page before you buy, as you would anyway.
  • It finds and checks. It doesn't buy anything or fill in anything for you.

Questions

Does it work on any shop?

On store sites you open in Safari, small shops included. It isn't tied to one marketplace. Some shops don't let a page be checked from outside, and those results say so.

Is it free?

No. Pro and Own key have a quick search, 5 a week; Ultra has the full search, 10 a week. On Free you can still ask about the page.

Does it buy anything for me?

No. It finds alternatives and checks their pages. You decide and buy yourself.

Does it work on iPad and Mac?

Yes, in Safari on iPhone, iPad and Mac.
