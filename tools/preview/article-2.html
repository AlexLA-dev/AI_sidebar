Japan's tech firms test the four-day week

Twelve Tokyo companies tried a shorter week for a year. Output per hour rose by almost a quarter — but only where meetings were cut first.

By Ken Aoki · 6 min read

Japan is famous for long hours, so when twelve software and design firms in Tokyo agreed to close every Friday for a year, few observers expected it to last. Nine of them are still on the shorter schedule.

Unlike earlier European pilots, the Japanese firms tracked output per hour rather than revenue. Across the group it rose by 23%, driven mostly by teams that halved their standing meetings before the trial began.

Not a universal fix

Three firms dropped out. Two said client support suffered on Fridays; one found that work simply moved into longer Thursdays. Employees there reported more stress, not less.

"The extra day only helped once we had decided what we would stop doing."

Researchers note the sample is small and self-selected. Still, the pattern matches other trials: the gains come from redesigning the work week, not from the day off itself.
